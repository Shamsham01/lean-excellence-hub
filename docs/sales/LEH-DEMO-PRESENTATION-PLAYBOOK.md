# LEH product demonstration playbook

Default format: a 45-minute live online meeting booked through Cal.com.

Audience: Continuous Improvement and Operational Excellence managers, operations directors, site directors, manufacturing leaders, and business improvement or transformation leaders.

Proposition to keep intact:

> Your framework. Your standards. Your way of working. One connected platform.

Use the product, not the marketing illustrations, once the introduction is over. The public homepage may orient the story for a moment. The demonstration itself is the signed-in application.

Do not invent ROI, savings, customer logos, testimonials or case-study performance. A benefit that has not been validated is described as awaiting validation.

Show only capabilities that are healthy in the demo environment. If a module fails its check on the day, skip it and say so. Do not present a broken screen as the product.

## Before the meeting

Follow [LEH-DEMO-PRESENTER-CHECKLIST.md](./LEH-DEMO-PRESENTER-CHECKLIST.md). Read the answers the attendee actually submitted in Cal.com. Those answers set the first five minutes. Do not treat a recommended question as asked unless it is on the published event. The answers are not a script to repeat back as if they were LEH claims.

## 0–5 minutes: Understand the customer

Ask what they use today for Gemba, 5S, actions, projects and performance improvement.

Ask where information is disconnected: spreadsheets, shared drives, email, slide decks, or separate point tools. Ask who chases the next step, and what the site leader cannot see.

Listen. Write their words down in their language. The follow-up email uses those words, not a generic problem statement.

## 5–8 minutes: Introduce LEH

Lean Excellence Hub is a multi-tenant Operational Excellence and Continuous Improvement platform.

One organisation can run its own framework and standards. Sites, areas and roles see the work they are responsible for. A finding does not end in the module where it was captured. It can become an owned action, a problem-solving case, an improvement project, a benefit that still needs validation, and a change to the standard.

Say explicitly: LEH supplies the engine. It does not replace the customer's doctrine.

## 8–25 minutes: One connected improvement story

Tell the story as Harbourfield Foods Group — Bodmin Bakery. The case is fictional: packaging film misalignment on Packing Line 1.

Walk the chain in the application, in this order:

1. **Gemba.** The observation is recorded against the line. It is evidence, not a project yet.
2. **Action.** Someone owns the immediate corrective action and a due date.
3. **Problem solving.** The recurring cause is investigated. The case is not closed by the assistant.
4. **Improvement project.** The sustained change is chartered.
5. **Benefits.** Any benefit is awaiting validation. Do not show it as verified, and do not attach a saving.
6. **Learning.** The useful change becomes a draft standard or a training expectation. A person publishes it.

Marketing illustrations use reference labels such as GEM-214, ACT-318, PS-077, PRJ-042, BEN-019 and CAP-014. If the live demo organisation uses different records, follow the records that exist. Do not pretend the marketing labels are the customer's data.

If that fictional chain is not already loaded in the approved demo organisation, do not create it during the call, and do not create it in a real customer organisation. Use the prepared fallback screenshots, say they are fallback, and continue the conversation. Loading Harbourfield, or resetting CookieWorks or any hosted organisation, is a separate approved data task. It is not part of booking setup and it is not something to run before a meeting.

## 25–35 minutes: Adaptability and intelligence

Show the items that match what the customer described, and only if that area is healthy:

- Maturity framework, including the option to start from a supplied standard or author their own
- 5S standard and audits
- Training or skills requirements
- Organisation and site structure
- Permissions and governance, including that sites are separate security boundaries

LeanAI, if shown:

- It sits in the context of the organisation, site, module and open record.
- It can find, explain and recommend.
- A person reviews. A person decides.
- LeanAI does not publish frameworks, close actions, grant access or purchase site capacity.

Do not demonstrate a module that has not passed QA in the environment you are using. The release smoke notes are the place to check status, not this playbook.

## 35–45 minutes: Customer fit and next steps

Discuss only what they raised:

- The business outcome they named in the first five minutes
- What a first site would need to start
- What would later extend to further sites
- Subscription shape, taken from the product rather than from memory

Implemented subscription shape, as of the billing catalogue:

- The site is the billable unit. Plans are in GBP.
- Essentials and Professional are self-service checkout plans.
- Enterprise is contact sales. It is not a self-service checkout plan.
- Founder Pilot is not a public plan. Do not offer it.

Open the onboarding plan step if a price is needed, and read what the screen shows. Do not recite figures from memory or from an old document. Do not invent a discount. Do not take payment on the call.

Close with one concrete next step they agree to: a single-site pilot, or a further technical meeting. Capture who does what. Use [LEH-DEMO-FOLLOW-UP-EMAIL.md](./LEH-DEMO-FOLLOW-UP-EMAIL.md) after the meeting. The product does not send that email.

Leave time for their questions. No obligation is part of the offer. Say that plainly.
